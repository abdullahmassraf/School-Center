Collaborative Research Project Overview

Course Learning Outcomes

This project will assess students on the entire range of course learning outcomes:

  1. Analyze argumentative texts.
  2. Evaluate evidence in various academic and non-academic contexts.
  3. Integrate primary and secondary sources in the construction of organized research.
  4. Formulate original and informed ideas about a selection of topics and contemporary issues. 
  5. Design arguments based on theoretical models.
  6. Collaborate with group members using effective interpersonal communication techniques.
  7. Use proper styles of documentation in formal essay contexts.

Wicked Problems in Ontario: Project Topics

Sign up for a group by going here.

1. Homeless Encampments
Since the COVID-19 pandemic, there has been a sharp increase in homeless encampments across Canada.  These encampments place already at-risk populations in greater danger of exposure to violence, drugs, and extreme weather conditions.  Encampments are also a source of public outrage amongst many communities. In fact, Ontario Premier Doug Ford has challenged communities to demand that he act, in writing, by using the notwithstanding clause.  What can be done at the municipal, provincial (Ontario), and federal (Canada) levels of government to address this on-going crisis? This news article from Global News can help you get started.

2. Exploitation of and Over-reliance on International Students in Post-Secondary Institutions
Under the Trudeau government, Canada has seen a very large influx of international students attending Ontario post-secondary institutions.  However, many of these students are sold false narratives about life in Canada, including elements related to cost-of-living, personal/financial support, and immigration pathways.  Recent efforts by the Ministry of Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship to restrict the number of international students permitted to study in Canada has resulted in a surge of anti-immigrant sentiments, racist scapegoat narratives, and a budgetary crisis for Ontario post-secondary institutions.  What can be done at the municipal, provincial (Ontario), and federal (Canada) levels of government to address this on-going crisis? What can post-secondary institutions do to help support students? This news article from the Toronto Star can help you get started.

3. Mental Health Crises in Post-Secondary Students
More and more students are facing tremendous pressure from home, work, and life while attending post-secondary studies.  Rising tuition rates, cost of living, lack of childcare, global political events, and many other factors have contributed to a mental health crisis amongst Ontario post-secondary institutions.  What can be done at the municipal, provincial (Ontario), and federal (Canada) levels of government to address this on-going crisis? What can post-secondary institutions do to help support students?  This press release from the Government of Ontario can help you get started.  

4. Health Care Crisis and Privatization
Ontario has long-struggled to retain enough family doctors and specialists to meet the health care needs of its population.  The notion of privatizing health care has been explored by the Ford government in recent years as the provincial government looks for solutions to this problem.  What can be done at the municipal, provincial (Ontario), and federal (Canada) levels of government to address this on-going crisis?  This website from the Ontario Medical Association can help you get started.

5. Climate Change
Ontario faces many complex issues related to climate change.  From the types of business and manufacturing industries in Ontario to climate change deniers, the province has an uphill battle ahead of itself. For example: "Using the 'Independent' Electricity System Operator (IESO) to defend the province’s commitment to burning more gas, the Ford government is pushing the federal government to water down its draft Clean Electricity Regulations by extending the 2035 deadline for phasing out gas plants" (Ontario Clean Air Alliance). What can be done at the municipal, provincial (Ontario), and federal (Canada) levels of government to address this on-going crisis? This website from the Canadian Climate Institute can help you get started.  

6. Opioid Epidemic
Opioids are an integral part of pain management in short-term and long-term patients and are a valuable resource when used appropriately.  However, a 2021 research study found that more than 1 in 4 deaths among young people in Canada were opioid-related (CBC News). While municipalities and cities are engaged in an ongoing war against opioid abuse, clearly, there is a large need to address this crisis more specifically.  What can be done at the municipal, provincial (Ontario), and federal (Canada) levels of government to address this on-going crisis?  This news article from Global News can help you get started. 

7. Censorship and Personal Freedoms
With the advent of AI and increasing issues surrounding misinformation, there has been much discussion about what policies and plans Ontario should implement to address the uncertainty of rapidly advancing technology.  In particular, bills like C-18 have caused much debate and outcry from Canadians based upon the Charter of Rights and Freedoms.  What can be done at the municipal, provincial (Ontario), and federal (Canada) levels of government to address this on-going crisis? This news release from the Government of Ontario can help you get started.

8. Rising Food Costs, Food Scarcity, and Price Regulations
Ontario has an on-going cost-of-living crisis that has resulted in a tremendous surge of food insecurity.  Food banks have declared states of emergency as they cannot keep up with the overwhelming demand for resources. In fact, Canada's "Grocery Code of Conduct" has attempted to address this issue with varying degrees of success. What can be done at the municipal, provincial (Ontario), and federal (Canada) levels of government to address this on-going crisis? This news article from Global News can help you get started.  

9. Decline of Pollinators
Ontario has been devoting a lot of time and resources to environmental conservation in recent years.  One aspect of these efforts is related to pollinators, which are insects and animals that carries pollen as part of the plant fertilization cycle.  However, with the use of dangerous pesticides, urban sprawl, and environmental issues, pollinators are at risk--and thus, so is our food supply.  What can be done at the municipal, provincial (Ontario), and federal (Canada) levels of government to address this on-going crisis?  This article from Canadian Geographic can help you get started.  

10. Gentrification and Housing Affordability 
As many people are learning as they try to get into the Ontario housing market, both the cost of affordable housing and rent prices have skyrocketed in recent years.  In fact, according to Statistics Canada, "almost one-quarter of a million Canadian households are on a wait list for social and affordable housing" (Statistics Canada).  What can be done at the municipal, provincial (Ontario), and federal (Canada) levels of government to address this on-going crisis? This article by Social Development Centre Waterloo Region can help you get started. 

Final Project Deliverable:

The multimedia format of the final deliverable is up to your group, but you should choose a mode that is appropriate for your topic and argument.   The purpose is to make the information you have gathered accessible and interesting for a wide audience of people with basic/general knowledge of your topic. 

Your group should have a clear purpose (inform, call to action, persuade) and aim to secure hypothetical support (emotional, legal, political, financial, etc.) from the audience--your professor and peers.  

Some suggestions for multimedia formats include:

  • A 10-15 minute "Dragons' Den" style pitch that you pre-record to present a solution to your topic
  • A blog (here are some  free platforms) that has at least 5 postings related to your topic
  • A 10-15 minute podcast on your topic
  • An "special issue" e-zine (Canva  is a good site to use) that focuses on your topic
  • A 10-15 minute documentary on your topic
  • A 10-15 minute infomercial  as a call to action for your topic
  • A mobile or desktop app

Project Stages:

This project will proceed in stages that are designed to help you learn to navigate the research process efficiently and to develop group work skills:

  1. MODULE 1: Sign up for a group by going here.
  2. MODULE 1: Establish initial contact with your group members.
    1. Connect with peers who are in your group via SLATE email (you can find the names of your group members in the sign up section (see Step 1).  

      Hint: Until you have connected the first time and determined how you wish to communicate, it will be easiest for one person to start a group email in SLATE.  You can go under Management --> Classlist to find the email addresses of your group members.  
    2. Establish how you will communicate and collaborate (a)synchronously over the next several weeks. 
    3. Complete the Group Contract Template (1%) and upload it to the  STAGE 1: Group Contract (1%) folder by the end of Module 1
  3. MODULE 3: Complete the Group Check Point 1 (2%) and upload it to the STAGE 2: Group Check Point #1 (2%) folder
  4. MODULE 4: Complete the Collaborative Group Proposal (15%) and upload it to the STAGE 3: Collaborative Group Proposal (15%) folder
  5. MODULE 6: Complete the Group Check Point 2 (2%)  and upload it to the STAGE 4: Group Check Point #2 (2%) folder
  6. MODULE 7: Complete the Collaborative Group Project (20%) and upload it to STAGE 5: Collaborative Group Project (20%) along with a copy of your Individual Group Assessment Form
