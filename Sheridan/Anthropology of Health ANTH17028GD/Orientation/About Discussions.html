ABOUT DISCUSSION (20%)

Discussion: There is one Discussion in this course worth 20% of your final grade. Discussion will range in its focus from subjective - asking you to relate the material to your own experiences – to objective - requiring you to critically evaluate academic theory and research findings. Discussion postings should answer the question posed in a clear, coherent fashion, using formal language, a logical thread of argument, proper paragraph structure, and correct citation and referencing (as applicable). Specific Discussion instructions will be provided in the accompanying Module.

Length: a good rule of thumb for your Discussion posts is around 500 words, but word length requirements will be clearly stated in the instructions accompanying the Discussion.

Peer response: As part of your Discussion, you will be required to construct a response to a peer’s discussion post. Your peer response should use formal language to briefly comment/expand on one or two specific points of interest from a peer’s Discussion posting. Responses are not critiques and should be respectful in tone. Length: 100-200 words.

Note: You cannot view other’s Discussions and Peer responses until you have posted your own Discussion post.

Tips: Compose Discussion and peer response in a Word document to prevent loss of work. When you are finished writing, cut and paste your work into the composition area—do not submit attachments.

Academic Integrity: Please ensure that your Discussion post abides by Sheridan’s Academic Integrity Policy and that any information referenced outside of your own experiences and ideas are appropriately cited using APA intext citations and bibliography. This is an individual assignment. Students are NOT permitted to use any generative AI programs, like ChatGPT, for any part of their assignment. 

Evaluation: You will find 'Discussions' under the 'Communication' tab on the toolbar above. Your Discussion and Peer Response will be graded with a rubric (located in the toolbar at the top of this page under 'Assessments'), so have a look before you begin your assignment. The Discussion topic can also be found in the toolbar. 

Value: 1 @ 20%.
