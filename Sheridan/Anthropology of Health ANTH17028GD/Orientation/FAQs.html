FAQs

How do I know I’m ready for an online class?

Take the quick ‘SELF-TEST: Am I ready for a distance education course?’, located in the Orientation Module.

How often do we meet for an actual class?

Anthropology of Health is taught completely online as an asynchronous online course, so we do not have scheduled face-to-face classes and will not meet virtually. You are expected to work through the course content at your own pace following the course schedule.

What do I have to do in this course?

Please review the course outline in detail (found in the Orientation Module)! It will outline the topics that are covered in each Module, how the material will be presented, how you will engage with the material and other students, and the types of evaluations that you will need to complete.

Detailed descriptions of each evaluation can be found in the Orientation Module, so please thoroughly read through all of the content in the Orientation Module, and complete the Self-Test and Academic Honesty quiz and tutorial before you begin Module 1.

If there is no physical class, what do I have to do each week?

During the first week of the course please review the Orientation Module, course syllabus, course calendar, and material in Module 1.

It is crucial that you follow the course schedule and thoroughly complete all material to be successful in this course. This means that you visit the course SLATE page every week, and:

  • Watch the video lectures and complete the accompanying activities and evaluations
  • Read the assigned readings
  • Review case studies
  • Participate in discussions
  • Complete all evaluations (module quizzes, discussions, and assignments)

Is there a midterm, or a final exam?

No, there is not. You will be required to complete a ‘Module Quiz’ at the end of each Module in order to proceed to the next Module. These quizzes are designed to assess your own comprehension of the material covered in each Module. Please ensure that you carefully and thoroughly review all Module material before you attempt the quiz.

 What if I was enrolled late?

Go over all the material in the Orientation Module. Get your bearings by consulting the Checklist (located in the toolbar). Start working your way through the lessons and activities in Module 1.

Is there a textbook?

No, Anthropology of Health is offered as a green course. In lieu of a physical textbook, selected readings that support each Module are provided as hyperlinks and e-documents in the SLATE course content. You will find a list of the required readings with each Module.

It is important that you read all of the required readings as you work your way through the course content. You will also be required to demonstrate your understanding of the readings as they relate to the lesson material through the evaluations in this course.

 Can I work ahead?

Yes and no. Only the first Modules will be available at the beginning of the course. The later Modules will be released as the course progresses. I want you to make sure that you do not get too far ahead, since some of the evaluations (Discussion Assignment) require you to write a response to your peer’s postings. If you post Discussion entries too far in advance, you might find that there are no peer entries to post a peer response to.

What are the due dates for specific evaluations?

Click on 'Course Schedule & Topical Outline' on the homepage and the course SLATE calendar.

Where do I find information about the evaluations, including instructions and rubrics?

Descriptions of each evaluation will be posted in the Orientation Module. Detailed instructions will be posted in the Module that each specific evaluation is associated with (if you do not complete the Module that is associated with a specific evaluation you will not be able to access the detailed instructions). Rubrics for each evaluation can either be found in the toolbar (at the top of your screen) or included in the assignment folder. 

Where do I post Discussion entries?

The Discussions area, under the 'Communications' tab at the top of your screen. Click  'Discussions' on the drop-down menu. This is important: do not upload attachments for the Discussions. However, to avoid losing your work, you are advised to write, edit, and save your discussion entries as a file on your own computer (Word document) and then cut and paste your text to the Discussion’s composition box. Do the same with peer responses. 

How do I submit my other evaluations?

All other evaluations are submitted to the appropriate 'Assignments' folder as a Word or PDF document. Click the ‘Assessments’ tab at the top of your screen, and then select 'Assignments' from the drop-down menu. You will see the names of each evaluation listed. Click on the name of the evaluation you want to submit and upload your document.

What is the penalty for late work?

Ten percent per day excluding weekends and statutory holidays. Assignments more than 5 days late will receive a failing grade.

Can I get an extension?

In certain situations I will provide an extension with a valid reason. All extension requests must be made well in advance of the due date (ideally a week before).  Everyone is busy so make sure you manage your time and your workload. 

How long do I have to wait for my assignments to be marked?

Usually around 2 weeks.

How quickly will my professor respond to an email query?

I will respond to all emails within 36 hours during regular business hours (Monday-Friday, 9am-5pm).
